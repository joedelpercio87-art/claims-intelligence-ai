# Denial Management Policy

This fictional operating policy describes an invented organization's internal workflow. It is not a statement of CMS, Medicare, Medicaid, HIPAA, payer, legal, clinical, or regulatory requirements.

## Denial monitoring

The denial operations team reviews denial counts and rates at least monthly by denial category, service category, program, provider, and processing period. Every report identifies the claim population, date basis, status definitions, exclusions, numerator, and denominator. Counts accompany rates so that small-volume variation is visible.

An operational review is opened when the overall denial rate changes by at least three percentage points from the immediately preceding comparable period, or when a category rate changes by at least five percentage points and has at least 20 claims in both periods. These are invented internal review thresholds, not evidence that a decision was incorrect.

## Root-cause investigation

The assigned reviewer samples records from the affected group and examines the denial code and category, claim history, relevant source documents, system edits, and applicable internal workflow records. The reviewer checks whether records were matched correctly and whether the comparison uses consistent populations and definitions.

Document confirmed observations separately from possible explanations. A pattern in aggregate results may guide sampling but does not establish a cause, error, medical necessity, misconduct, or responsibility. Escalate unresolved technical or process questions to the relevant operations owner.

## Remediation tracking

When a process issue is substantiated through record review, the manager documents the evidence, scope, accountable owner, corrective action, due date, validation method, and follow-up measurement. Track proposed, approved, implemented, and verified actions separately. Do not close an action solely because an aggregate rate later changes.

If review does not substantiate an issue, document what was checked, the sample and limitations, and why no remediation was assigned. Retain open questions when the available evidence is incomplete.

## Appeal and reprocessing review

Route an appeal or reprocessing request through the organization's designated review queue. A qualified human reviewer checks the request, supporting records, prior decision history, and applicable internal procedures. Record the disposition and rationale in the designated system. An aggregate trend or automated score cannot decide an individual appeal or trigger claim reprocessing by itself.

## Escalation and reporting

The denial operations lead reviews material trends with the claims operations manager, including magnitude, duration, volume, affected workflows, evidence reviewed, uncertainty, and actions. Reports intended for management retain provenance and distinguish observed measures from interpretation.
