# Provider Operations Escalation Policy

This fictional operating policy describes an invented organization's internal workflow. It is not a statement of CMS, Medicare, Medicaid, HIPAA, payer, legal, clinical, or regulatory requirements.

## When to open an operational review

An operations manager may open a provider-level review when a provider's denial rate is at least ten percentage points above the comparable service-category rate for two consecutive periods and the provider has at least 30 claims in each period. The manager may also review a sustained increase in prior-authorization-related denials or a notable shift in claim volume when the comparison population and data quality have been checked.

These thresholds identify a workflow for review only. Unusual provider-level denial patterns alone do not establish fraud, abuse, provider intent, improper care, or wrongdoing. Do not describe a provider as culpable based on a rate, rank, or automated flag.

## Review and evidence

The reviewer checks the time period, denominator, service and program mix, claim status definitions, provider identifier mapping, data completeness, and comparison group. The reviewer examines a balanced sample of affected and unaffected claims, including source records and relevant authorization or processing history where available.

Record each observed fact with its source and date. Keep hypotheses separate, identify alternative explanations, and state what evidence is missing. If the pattern is explained by a data or categorization issue, document the correction and assess whether prior reports need amendment.

## Documentation and outreach workflow

The manager opens a case with the trigger, measures, denominator, comparison method, sample selection, records reviewed, limitations, and next steps. If operational clarification is appropriate, an authorized liaison prepares a neutral information request through an approved channel. The request describes the records or workflow question without implying misconduct.

Outreach is reviewed by the operations manager before sending. The case log records the purpose, reviewer, date, recipient role, response, documents received, and resulting action. Any material claim-level reconsideration remains within the established human review process.

## Escalation and closure

Escalate unresolved data-quality or workflow questions to the claims operations manager. Further organizational review requires documented evidence and approval through the separate internal escalation process. Close the operational case only after the review outcome, evidence limits, any corrective action, owner, and follow-up date are recorded.
