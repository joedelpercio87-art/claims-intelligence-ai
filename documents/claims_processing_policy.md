# Claims Processing Policy

This fictional operating policy describes an invented organization's internal workflow. It is not a statement of CMS, Medicare, Medicaid, HIPAA, payer, legal, clinical, or regulatory requirements.

## Purpose and scope

This policy sets internal expectations for receiving, checking, routing, and monitoring synthetic claim records. It applies to operations staff and managers who review processing performance. It does not determine coverage, payment entitlement, clinical appropriateness, or legal compliance.

## Processing-time expectations

For routine complete claims, the operations team uses a five-calendar-day internal target from receipt to an initial processing outcome. This is an internal demonstration target only. A claim awaiting information or a documented system hold is tracked in its own work state; the hold reason and start date must be recorded so elapsed time is visible.

Managers review median and 90th-percentile processing time, open inventory, age of oldest work, and volume by work state at least weekly. Reports must identify the date range, claim population, exclusions, and whether elapsed time includes documented holds.

## Monitoring thresholds

An operational review is opened when either the weekly median exceeds five calendar days or the 90th-percentile exceeds ten calendar days for two consecutive reporting periods. A queue whose oldest unheld item exceeds ten calendar days also receives review. These thresholds are internal monitoring triggers, not proof of a service failure or cause.

The reviewer checks whether the change is broad or concentrated by intake channel, work state, service category, program, and system queue. Comparisons must include volume and denominator, and small groups must be identified before making a rate comparison.

## Deterioration and escalation

When a threshold is met, the operations lead records the affected measures and period, assigns an owner, checks queue routing and staffing or system incident records, and sets a review date. If work is at risk of remaining unprocessed, the lead escalates the operational capacity issue to the claims operations manager on the same business day.

Any proposed process change is reviewed by an accountable manager, documented with its reason and effective date, and monitored for unintended effects. A metric change alone does not authorize an individual claim decision.

## Documentation expectations

Every review records the source report, query or report version, date range, numerator and denominator, known exclusions, observations, hypotheses as hypotheses, evidence checked, actions, owner, due date, and closure rationale. Preserve the work-state history and avoid replacing an earlier observation with a later interpretation.
