# Claims Audit and Reconciliation Policy

This fictional operating policy describes an invented organization's internal workflow. It is not a statement of CMS, Medicare, Medicaid, HIPAA, payer, legal, clinical, or regulatory requirements.

## Sampling and reconciliation

An audit reviewer defines the question, population, time window, selection method, and sample size before examining records. The reviewer records whether the sample is random, stratified, or targeted and explains the limitations of that method. Targeted samples may help understand a pattern but must not be presented as representative of the full population without support.

For each sampled claim, reconcile the claim header, claim lines, adjudication status, amount fields, denial fields, linked authorization when relevant, and associated source records. Record missing, inconsistent, or unavailable fields without substituting assumptions. Reconcile aggregate counts to the source extract and note duplicate handling and exclusions.

## Evidence requirements

An audit note identifies the source system or file, extraction date, query or report version, record references, date fields used, reviewer, and method. Preserve the original evidence and any subsequent correction history. For each conclusion, cite the records and rule or internal procedure applied, including contrary evidence and unresolved questions.

Separate direct observations from interpretations and confirmed findings. A rate difference, anomaly, or association is a signal for review, not a substantiated finding on its own. The strength of a conclusion must not exceed the quality, completeness, and scope of the evidence reviewed.

## Review and quality control

A second reviewer checks material reconciliations and any proposed consequential action. Differences between reviewers are documented and resolved by the audit lead. Reviewers preserve version history for working papers, calculations, and management summaries so another reviewer can reproduce the process.

## Findings and follow-up

The audit lead labels outcomes as confirmed observation, unresolved question, or substantiated finding under this fictional organization's internal review process. The lead records scope, basis, limitations, reviewer approval, owner, action, target date, and closure evidence. Operational anomalies must not be described as legal, clinical, fraud, abuse, intent, or wrongdoing findings without appropriate independent evidence and authorized review.
