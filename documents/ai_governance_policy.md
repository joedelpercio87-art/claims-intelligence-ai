# AI Decision Support Governance Policy

This fictional operating policy describes an invented organization's internal workflow. It is not a statement of CMS, Medicare, Medicaid, HIPAA, payer, legal, clinical, or regulatory requirements.

## Decision-support boundary

AI tools may organize information, retrieve approved internal reference material, summarize records, and calculate or display descriptive measures from authorized systems. AI output is decision support only. A qualified human remains responsible for consequential operational decisions.

AI must not autonomously deny or adjudicate a claim, make a medical-necessity decision, determine coverage, or accuse a member or provider of fraud, abuse, intent, fault, or wrongdoing. A model output, retrieval result, similarity score, or statistical pattern cannot by itself substantiate such a conclusion.

## Human review

Before an AI-supported recommendation affects a claim, provider, or member, an authorized reviewer checks the underlying records, applicable internal procedure, source provenance, limitations, and alternative explanations. The reviewer records whether the recommendation was accepted, changed, or rejected and the evidence supporting the final action.

AI output must not bypass established queues, approval steps, appeal review, or correction controls. Uncertain, conflicting, or incomplete evidence is escalated to the appropriate human owner. The reviewer can decline to act on an AI suggestion.

## Minimum necessary context

Provide only the fields needed for the specific task. Prefer aggregate or de-identified synthetic information for development and demonstration. Do not include credentials, unnecessary personal details, or protected health information in prompts, retrieval indexes, logs, or test fixtures. Separate structured operational evidence from retrieved policy evidence and retain their distinct sources.

## Evidence provenance and factual language

AI-supported reports identify which facts came from a database metric, which passages came from a policy document, and which statements are interpretation. Retrieved policy passages must retain source filename, title, section, and text. Fictional demonstration policies must always be represented as fictional internal examples, never as external regulatory or legal requirements.

Use language such as “the records show” for a directly supported observation and label hypotheses or interpretations clearly. Do not turn association into causation. If sources conflict or evidence is missing, disclose the limitation rather than filling the gap with model-generated assumptions.

## Auditability and oversight

Maintain an auditable record of the tool or model version, request purpose, input references, retrieved sources, output, human reviewer, decision, and material edits, subject to the organization's approved retention practices. Periodically test retrieval accuracy, access boundaries, failure handling, and the ability to reproduce outputs. Disable or escalate a workflow when it produces unsupported claims or cannot preserve source provenance.
